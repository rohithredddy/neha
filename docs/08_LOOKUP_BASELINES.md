**The ligand's heavy-atom count, with no protein and no training, matches every published model on
ID** (DrugBAN 97.6, GraphBAN 97.8, FusionDTI 98.3) and beats FusionDTI on p_ood_25. Labels come from
Vina docking thresholds, and docked affinity scales with ligand size. In ID, 90.6% of rows belong
to a ligand whose label never varies across proteins (ad-hoc check, to be scripted). Protein
identity carries almost no label signal. Caveats: our ID folds are not the paper's (unpublished),
and the FusionDTI OOD numbers in the paper may be averaged over split seeds we do not have; the
p25/p33 paper values are from 06_RESEARCH_UPDATE §1.

Exact-sequence lookup covers almost no test pairs, so it says little about the benchmark as a
whole. Where it applies (57 p_ood_33 pairs), copying train sites beats ExplainBind's reported
BRHR@1 55.6 / @5 74.6 — small n, but the leak is real. The next residue baseline must be **homology
transfer** (align to nearest train sequence), which covers the 62–68% of OOD test pairs sharing a
UniProt ID with train.
