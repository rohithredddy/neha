## Findings that affect method or evaluation
1. **ID negatives are real co-crystal complexes.** In `affinity`, 99.96% of label-0 rows have
   binding sites. They are PDB complexes whose *docked* affinity is weak (> −5). In the OOD
   splits, negatives have **no** sites (random docked negatives, 1:1 balanced). So the ID binary
   task is "strong vs. weak docked affinity among crystal binders", and the OOD binary task is
   "binder vs. decoy". The paper should state this.
2. **Duplicates in ID.** There are 6,308 (fasta, smiles) groups that occur more than once: 371
   with conflicting labels, 5,886 with differing site sets (different PDB entries of the same
   complex). A random pair-level CV would leak, so a grouped split is mandatory.
3. **p_ood_25 is largely a length split.** Train L ≤ 491; val/test L ≥ 553 (median ≈ 990), and
   42–43% of test pairs have L > 1024. 136 test positives have a binding residue at index ≥ 1024.
   Random-ranking BRHR@5 is 27.2% on train vs **7.3% on test**, because longer proteins make the
   metric harder by chance.
4. **Protein-OOD splits share UniProt IDs.** 67.6% (p25) and 62.2% (p33) of test pairs have a
   UniProt ID that appears in train. 10.6% of p_ood_33 test pairs have a FASTA string identical to
   a train FASTA. The splits are by construct sequence, not by protein. This needs MMseqs2
   confirmation before any claim (M3).
5. **Random-ranking baseline (analytic, annotated positives).** ID: BRHR@1 4.5%, @3 12.6%,
   @5 20.0%. Published FusionDTI BRHR@5 = 35.6%, i.e. +15.6 points over chance.
6. **Binding sites differ across ligands of the same protein (same FASTA).** Mean Jaccard of
   `Overall` sites = 0.44 (ID), with ~1% identical. 45% of ID annotated positives belong to a
   protein with ≥ 2 annotated ligands. N2 (ligand-agnostic ceiling) is therefore well-posed and
   testable.
7. **Type prevalence among ID annotated positives** (fraction of pairs with ≥ 1 residue of that
   type): vdW 99.7%, H-bond 94.7%, hydrophobic 83.3%, salt 48.2%, π–π 32.9%, cation–π 12.9%.
   n⁺ (binding residues) median 13, p10–p90 = 8–21, max 38.
8. **Long proteins in ID.** 1.9% of ID rows have L > 1024; 249 positives have a binding residue at
   index ≥ 1024.

## Consequences (logged in DECISIONS.md)
- Evaluation must **never** use labels to choose a crop window (spec §7 crop-by-annotation is
  train-only). Eval uses full-length sequences, with ESM embeddings computed in overlapping
  windows for L > 1022.
- Report BRHR together with the per-split random baseline, and stratify by length.
- Findings 3–4 are benchmark-audit material for the paper (N2 section). Verify them with MMseqs2 first.
